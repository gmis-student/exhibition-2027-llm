AGIs vs LLMs

ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Gemini. All of these are known as AI, artificial intelligence. But what separates them from AGIs, artificial general intelligences?

See, all those AIs are actually LLMs—large language models. What this means is that their functionality is algorithmic, not based on an actual understanding of what you are saying. The training data LLMs use is a lot like the books you might read to learn, but instead of understanding, an LLM will break words down into ‘tokens’ and search for patterns. Then, based on these patterns, it will predict answers.

An AGI, on the other hand, doesn’t actually exist yet. It’s what you think of when presented with the term ‘AI’—something that actually understands what you tell it, and can function similarly to a human. These are what, theoretically, would gain sentience and take over the world—not LLMs, which are essentially data regurgitators.

But LLMs are very much flawed. Them being essentially data regurgitators means that they will just go off whatever is most common, and if it doesn’t exist? They’ll invent it. You don’t want the LLM to invent facts in your essay—another reason to write it yourself.
