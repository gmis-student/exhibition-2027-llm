LLMs vs AGIs

ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Gemini. All of these are known as AI, artificial intelligence. But what separates them from AGIs, artificial general intelligences?

See, all those AIs are actually LLMs—large language models. What this means is that their functionality is algorithmic, not based on an actual understanding of what you are saying. The training data LLMs use is a lot like the books you might read to learn, but instead of understanding, an LLM will break words down into ‘tokens’ and search for patterns. Then, based on these patterns, it will predict answers.

An AGI, on the other hand, doesn’t actually exist yet. It’s what you think of when presented with the term ‘AI’—something that actually understands what you tell it, and can function similarly to a human. These are what, theoretically, would gain sentience and take over the world—not LLMs, which are essentially data regurgitators.

But LLMs are very much flawed. Them being essentially data regurgitators means that they will just go off whatever is most common, and if it doesn’t exist? They’ll invent it. You don’t want the LLM to invent facts in your essay—another reason to write it yourself.


Responsible Usage of genAI

"I want AI to do my laundry and dishes so that I can do art and writing, not for AI to do my art and writing so that I can do my laundry and dishes."

This quote, which has pervaded the entire Internet, essentially sums up most of the topic for this exhibition—what you should be using AI for, and what you shouldn’t be.

You should not be using it to create artwork or writing. You should not be using it for making the entirety of your code (vibe coding), or making all your music for you. You should not even be using it to generate your essays or solving your homework for you. Instead, try using it to look for sources for your writing (with, of course, plenty of cross-checking and fact verification) or learning the concepts behind things so you can do it yourself.

In fact, according to a study by MIT, those who use AI to generate their work for them display consistently less understanding of the topic and inability to quote their ‘own’ work compared to those who use it for sources or do the entire thing on their own, making it rather counterproductive to the idea behind giving essay-type work to use.

Besides this, due to the way LLMs function, (see LLMs vs AGIs,) as well as the fact that they essentially scour the entire Internet for art/writing to use, before promptly being used to generate images and texts used to sideline said artists/writers, it’s rather akin to plagiarism and a hundred billion copyright violations as well. Not good!
